\textbf{Background:} Diabetes affects over 500 million people worldwide and strains healthcare systems. AI and machine learning (ML) show promise for prevention, diagnosis, and management, but combining wearable sensors, continuous glucose monitors, and retinal imaging creates technical and practical challenges that remain incompletely addressed.

\textbf{Objective:} This review examines key obstacles facing AI/ML in diabetes care: data inconsistency across devices and populations, poor model performance outside training conditions, unfair outcomes for certain patient groups, lack of transparency in model decisions, and deployment barriers in resource-limited settings. We pay particular attention to underserved communities and low- and middle-income countries, where the gap between research promise and clinical reality is widest.

\textbf{Methods:} We reviewed 361 papers published between 2018 and 2025 from SciSpace, Google Scholar, ArXiv, PubMed, and npj Digital Medicine. Our analysis covered the methods researchers use, the types of data they work with, how they combine multiple data sources, and what limitations they report.

\textbf{Results:} AI/ML systems perform well on standard test data but struggle on new populations, settings, or devices. Deep learning dominates but reproducibility is poor: studies rely on similar datasets, rarely use external validation, and omit key details. Multimodal fusion can improve accuracy but missing data and temporal alignment pose challenges. Models consistently perform worse for women, patients with poor glucose control, and underrepresented groups. In resource-limited settings, infrastructure, cost, privacy, and lack of local validation add further obstacles.

\textbf{Conclusions:} Equitable AI-driven diabetes care requires testing on diverse populations, reporting subgroup performance, using privacy-preserving methods like federated learning, and considering cost and infrastructure from the start. Without these changes, AI risks widening rather than closing health gaps.
