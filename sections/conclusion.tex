\section{Conclusion}

AI offers real promise: deep learning can match specialists at retinopathy detection, predict glucose swings, and integrate data at scale \cite{zhang2025systematic, sun2021dualpathway, lee2024multimodal}; several tools have regulatory approval and trials show benefit \cite{wolf2024access, boucher2024implementation}. This review also documents a substantial gap between benchmarks and clinical reality. Models that excel on test data routinely fail on new populations, devices, or settings. Prioleau et al. \cite{prioleau2025deep} found poor conceptual reproducibility across datasets and worse performance for women and for patients with poor glucose control—those who need help most. Retinal screening shows similar variability \cite{zhang2025systematic, olusanya2024mitigating}.

These reflect how research is conducted: overrepresented training data, metrics that hide subgroup disparities, and validation rarely outside original context \cite{wang2024ai, prioleau2025deep}. Low-resource settings expose the weaknesses: infrastructure, cost, data scarcity, and workforce barriers \cite{desai2025barriers, eze2022sustainability, ghosh2025artificial}; LMICs bear a disproportionate burden but remain nearly invisible in training data \cite{sun2022idf, olusanya2024mitigating, raj2024federated}. Without deliberate inclusion, AI risks concentrating benefits among those already well-served \cite{haider2024algorithmic, wang2024ai}.

The path forward requires diverse data with explicit targets \cite{bai2024federated, fahmy2025exploring}, fairness and domain adaptation in model development \cite{fahmy2025exploring, chen2024crossmodality}, and evaluation that includes subgroups, external validation, calibration, and uncertainty \cite{prioleau2025deep, jacobs2023artificial, liu2025scoping}. Federated learning can include underrepresented populations without sharing raw data \cite{bai2024federated, raj2024federated}; transfer learning and domain adaptation can help where local data are scarce, with validation to avoid negative transfer \cite{chen2024crossmodality, zhou2023population}. Regulatory frameworks, education, and economic evidence for resource-constrained settings are needed \cite{mackenzie2023diabetes, khalifa2024artificial, bahmani2025achieving, guan2023artificial, ghosh2025artificial}.

The measure of AI in diabetes care should be whether it improves outcomes for people who need help: systems reliable across populations and settings, fair across demographics and disease severity, interpretable, and accessible where resources are limited. Diabetes affects hundreds of millions and will affect more \cite{sun2022idf}. Realizing AI's potential requires prioritizing equity and robustness alongside performance and serving all who need care, not only those easiest to reach.
